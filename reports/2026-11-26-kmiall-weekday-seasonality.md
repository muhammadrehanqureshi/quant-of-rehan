# KMIALL weekday seasonality, five years of sessions

Built 2026-11-26 from the PSX daily archive panel (full OHLCV, one row per scrip per session). Window: 2021-11-26 to 2026-11-25, 5.0 years, 1239 sessions, 270 KMIALL scrips.

Source run: GitHub Actions run 45779397294 of the `backtest` workflow, task `seasonality`. The panel itself is gitignored by law, so the numbers travel as this document and its JSON evidence file.

## The question, and the answer

A session counts as GREEN for a scrip when its close is at least 1.5 percent above the previous session's close. The question was which scrips "always go green" on a specific weekday, and there should be concrete evidence for the claim.

**No scrip is always green on any weekday.** Every one of the 1350 scrip and weekday pairs tested has at least one session of that weekday that failed the 1.5 percent bar. The best single pair is **FECM on FRIDAY**: green 101 of 218 times (46.3%), and not green on 117 sessions of that weekday.

That is the honest reading of five years: the data does not contain a day on which any scrip always goes green. What it does contain is a ranked list of the pairs whose green rate is furthest above their own base rate, with the counts, the dates and a significance test attached so you can judge each one yourself.

## How this is measured

* The bar: close to close return of at least +1.5 percent.
* Window: the last 5 years of the panel, measured back from its final session.
* A pair is tested only with at least 100 sessions of that weekday, and a scrip only with at least 500 sessions in the window, so a newly listed scrip cannot look like a pattern.
* Base rate: the same scrip's green rate across all weekdays. The weekday has to beat that, otherwise it is just a volatile scrip.
* p value: one-sided exact binomial tail, P(at least this many green days | the scrip's own base rate). Small n cannot flatter a row.
* 95 percent interval: Wilson score interval on the weekday rate.
* Multiple testing: 1350 pairs were tested, so the Bonferroni threshold is 3.70e-05. 0 pair(s) clear it. Benjamini-Hochberg q values are reported beside the p values for a less severe read.
* Split halves: the window is cut in two at each scrip's median session date, and both halves' green rates are reported, so a rate that lives only in one half is visible as the noise it probably is.

## What this does not prove

* A weekday rate is a description of the past window, not a forecast. The window is the window the pattern was found in.
* 1350 tests at once means some pairs look strong by construction. The p and q columns are there to be read before the rate is.
* Surviving scrips change: the KMIALL list of today is applied to the whole window, so scrips that left the list are missing and scrips that joined late carry short histories.
* Thin scrips cannot be traded at the price that printed. A green session in a scrip with no volume is a print, not a fill, and PSX price limits can freeze an exit.
* Costs are not charged here at all. A 1.5 percent green session is a gross move; the repository's own law charges about 1.1 percent round trip before any tax.
